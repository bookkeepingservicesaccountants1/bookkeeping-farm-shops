Bookkeeping for Farm Shops and Delis: Own Produce, Bought-In Lines and Café Corners

A farm shop sells three businesses under one roof: what the farm grows, what it buys in, and what the café corner serves. Blended into one till figure, none of the three can be judged.

Own produce versus bought-in lines

Eggs, veg and meat from the farm itself carry production costs — feed, seed, labour — rather than a purchase invoice. Value own produce transferred to the shop consistently (a standard transfer price per line works) so the shop margin and the farm margin are both honest. Bought-in lines — cheeses, preserves, gifts — are ordinary resale stock.

The café corner

A café or tea room corner has different margins and different waste. Till departments per area keep café sales, shop food and non-food apart; one blended figure hides the corner that quietly loses money on quiet weekdays.

Seasonal staff and harvest rhythms

Picking, packing and peak-season shop staff move with the harvest and the holidays. Seasonal wages through payroll, casual labour records kept properly, and the Christmas and lambing peaks planned as cost seasons — the books should show the same rhythm the yard does.

Waste, markdowns and honesty boxes

Perishables get marked down or wasted daily. A simple waste log per department keeps stock honest and shows which lines consistently over-order. Honesty-box and farm-gate sales away from the main till are still shop income — a weekly count into the record settles it.
